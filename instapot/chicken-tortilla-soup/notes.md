# Chicken Tortilla Soup — Notes

Reasoning behind the non-obvious choices, and the places this can go wrong.

## Moong dal

Dried split legumes in a sealed dry jar don't spoil — a year on the shelf is fine, they just cook slower, which pressure cooking erases. Check for musty smell, moisture clumping, or small dark beetles and webbing (bruchid weevils are the only real failure mode).

It's flavor-neutral, dissolves into the broth, and adds protein and fiber invisibly. **Cap it at 3/4 cup** or the soup becomes stew. Split yellow melts completely; whole green mung with skins stays as soft distinct beans.

## Liquid

1/2 cup dry dal absorbs roughly 3/4–1 cup of liquid. Against that, 3 lb of thighs release about a cup as they cook and the spinach (91% water) gives back another half cup. Net absorption is near zero — hence 6.5 cups rather than a big upward adjustment.

What the dal actually does is **thicken** rather than absorb, via shed starch. Extra broth only partly offsets that.

Two things that matter more than the starting number:

- **No evaporation in a sealed pot.** The stovetop instinct of "add extra, it'll cook down" overshoots badly in pressure cooking. Whatever goes in stays in.
- **You can thin at the end; you can't unthin.** The tortilla slurry is a second thickener, so the real consistency isn't knowable until both are in.

Expect it noticeably thicker the next day — dal and masa keep absorbing in the fridge. Thin leftovers with broth on reheat; that's normal.

## Spinach goes in *before* pressure

Both goals point the same way: it should vanish. Twelve minutes at pressure reduces the leaves to soft flecks that read as herbs. Added after, you get intact slippery strands — which is the exact texture kids pick out.

Chlorophyll does convert to pheophytin under heat and acid (the olive-drab shift), but this soup is already red-orange from fire-roasted tomatoes and ancho, so drab green disappears into it. Cost is some folate and vitamin C, which are heat-labile — a small loss against thighs, dal, beans, and three vegetables.

Fresh spinach is absurdly bulky before it wilts; pack it under the liquid rather than mounding it. Frozen takes no headspace.

## Corn and beans go in *after*

Frozen corn is blanched before freezing and canned corn is fully cooked — both are already done. Twelve minutes at pressure turns them soft and washes out the sweetness. Same for canned black beans.

Frozen corn beats canned here: more pop, sweeter, no can liquid.

## The tortilla slurry

This is the flavor signature — it's what makes the soup taste like tortilla soup rather than chicken vegetable soup.

- **Use cooked soup liquid, not plain broth.** The soup liquid is already seasoned, fatty, and tomato-rich; plain broth dilutes and thickens simultaneously, working against itself. The reserved cup of plain broth is for *thinning only*, a separate job.
- **Blend it hot.** Corn starch needs heat to gelatinize; cooling gives a gritty slurry that won't thicken.
- **Blender safety:** hot liquid in a sealed blender builds steam pressure and will blow the lid. Remove the center cap, cover with a folded towel, start low. An immersion blender sidesteps it entirely.
- Ladle mostly liquid, not chunks — you don't want puréed carrots and corn. A little onion adds body.

### Alternatives, ranked

1. **Masa harina** — 3 Tbsp whisked into 1/2 cup cool broth until lump-free, stirred in, simmered 5 min. Same corn, same starch (tortillas are pressed and griddled masa). No blender, perfectly smooth, nothing to find.
2. **Blending tortillas** — as written.
3. **Torn and simmered** — tear to 1/4", hold on Sauté ~10 min stirring. Works, but grainier, and stray soggy pieces survive. Soggy tortilla is a texture kids flag.

Make the baked tortilla strips regardless — that's a separate job, and it's where the crunch and toasted corn aroma come from.

## Burn error prevention

1. Deglaze the skillet thoroughly and scrape everything in. Sautéing off-pot already removes most of the risk since there's no scorched fond welded to the element.
2. Plain liquid on the bottom of the pot, nothing starchy sitting against it.
3. Stir the dal into the liquid rather than leaving it piled.
4. **Tomatoes on top, unstirred.** Acidic tomato against the element is the classic trigger. Free insurance.

Erring slightly wet also cuts scorch risk.

## Natural release

Dal foams. A hard vent will spit. Ten to fifteen minutes natural release isn't optional here.

## Whole spices

Blooming whole cumin seed in hot oil is a straight upgrade — cumin is core to this profile. Whole seed gives toasty and nutty; ground gives earthy body. You want both, at reduced doses of each, hence 1 tsp whole + 2 tsp ground rather than the full 1 Tbsp ground.

Toasted coriander seed is citrusy and plays well with the lime at the end.

**Skip mustard seeds.** The popping brown-mustard note is unmistakably South Asian and pulls the soup sideways. Not ruinous, just confused.

## Lime before salt

Acid makes food taste saltier. Salt first and you'll oversalt, then the lime pushes it over. This step is the difference between flat and bright — not optional.

## Capacity

An 8 qt handles this as written. On a 6 qt, drop to 5 cups broth and add corn and beans after depressurizing — legumes foam, and Instant Pot's guidance is half-full max for beans.
